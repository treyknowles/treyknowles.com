The question I get asked most often is: "How do you decide whether to join an early-stage company?"

After doing it a few times — once badly, a couple of times well — I have a cleaner framework. It comes down to three things: founder conviction, product-market signal, and GTM timing.

## Founder conviction

Early-stage GTM is mostly about translating the founder's vision into language the market understands. If the founder doesn't have deep conviction about why their product matters and who it's for, your job becomes impossible. You're building on sand.

What I look for: can the founder explain in one sentence who the product is for and what specific pain it solves? Not a TAM slide. Not a vision deck. A single, crisp sentence that makes a developer say "oh, that's exactly my problem."

## Product-market signal

Before I join, I want to see some signal that real people use the product because they want to, not because a salesperson called them 47 times.

This might look like GitHub stars (imperfect, but a signal). It might look like an active Discord. It might look like a few case studies where customers actually volunteered to participate. What it doesn't look like is a "pipeline" built by an outsourced SDR farm.

## GTM timing

This one is underrated. There's a window — usually between initial product traction and the first enterprise contracts — where an early GTM hire can actually move the needle. Too early, and you're just debugging a product with no customers. Too late, and the playbook is already written.

The sweet spot is when developers have found the product on their own and are telling colleagues about it, but the company doesn't yet know how to accelerate that motion intentionally.

---

I've gotten this wrong before. I joined one company that had all three criteria on paper but the founder's conviction was borrowed — they were building what the market said they wanted rather than what they deeply believed in. The product shipped on time but nobody loved it. A good reminder that intuition still matters.
